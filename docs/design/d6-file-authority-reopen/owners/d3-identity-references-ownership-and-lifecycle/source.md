# D3 Identity, References, Ownership, and Lifecycle

Candidate status: D6-FA-r01; partial coordinated candidate; not accepted, not activated, not implemented. Fixed-S D3 wire11/Result9 and saved v9/v10 decisions remain historical compatibility sources. This afterimage defines D3 wire12 for new decisions and may activate only with D6-FA-r01 plus the later D4/D5/D7/D8/D9/D10 owner afterimages under fresh joint review. It authorizes no product implementation, release, or A2.

## 1. Scope, frozen inputs, and non-goals

D3 remains the sole owner of:

- identity algebra for WorkspaceRef, NodeRef, ResourceRef, and AnnotationRef;
- logical Node parent/order semantics;
- live, trashed, and tombstoned lifecycle for Node/Resource/Annotation;
- Locator, anchor, reference lifecycle, and copy/fork/continue/import identity rules;
- D3 operation request, receipt, resolver outcome, and fail-closed families.

D6 solely owns the current producers and persistence semantics: `DecisionKey/2`, `CommitDomain/2`, `ReplicaEpoch`, `ChangeId/1`, and `Frontier/2`; the complete managed/external `SourceVersion/2`; current `SourceObservation/1` and narrow `SourceVersionRef/1`; `SourceStamp/1`, `SourceRevisionPlan/1`, resolution-only ConflictInstallInput/1 and SourceRevisionPlan/2 (§10.1), closed `RevisionTokenSource/2`, `RevisionTokenBinding/2`, and tag `d6_source_revision/2`; `ObservationScope/2`; `DependencyProof/2` with exactly fourteen `DependencyKey/2` kinds (`source|lifecycle|placement_range|ref_inbound|relation_incidence|calendar_scope|registry|temporal_rules|authorization|foreign_binding|query_scan|replica_registry|conflict_record|execution_resource`), where `placement_range` has exactly nine `StructureRange` variants (`live_children|trash_children|trash_roots|ancestor_chain|subtree|owner_resources|owner_annotations|reply_closure|restore_membership`); `OwnerInputBinding/2`, `InputDescriptor/2`, `PreparedIntent/2`, `D3DecisionCompanion/2`, and `InstallationNotice/2`; new-FA `ContentCompletionProof/3` and the separate historical `/2` decoder; current `ConflictRecord/2 + Frontier/2` and historical `ConflictRecord/1 + Frontier/1`, while `ConflictKey/1`, `ConflictId`, and the `D6-ConflictKey/1` hash domain remain unchanged; plus actual file installation, P seal, publication/recovery, and execution-resource/control-responsibility carriers, including `ExecutionResponsibilityRecord` where the contract uses it. D3 consumes only these exact types, never redefines same-name wire/schema, never changes the original opaque D3 Locator/selector token shape, and never creates a second writable truth.

D2 exact source, D4 typed schema/relations, D5 structure, D7 Query/Action, D8 editor, D9 conversion, and D10 external execution remain with their owners.

## 2. Design goals

1. Physical path, index row, mtime, digest, and sync-provider identifier are not content identity.
2. Multiple registered replicas of one logical Workspace preserve the same durable refs while allowing ordinary offline content operations independently.
3. Ordinary replica content qualification is separate from global execution responsibility.
4. Parent/order, lifecycle, tombstones, and no-reuse facts are portable and never exist only in discardable I.
5. replica_local narrows only the semantic proof scope of this D3 operation; every D3 identity/structure/lifecycle author installation still requires WriteProtection=strict. It never implies one global current state or a complete D7 set.
6. managed_atomic retains complete-closure, complete-range, and strong recovery semantics for operations that need them.
7. External changes, placeholders, partial transport, and ABA never regain old continuity merely because hashes match.
8. Saved-decision replay and current-source state are always separate.

## 3. Identity algebra and terminology domains

WorkspaceId, AuthorityInstanceId, NodeId, ResourceId, and AnnotationId remain canonical lowercase RFC 4122 UUIDv4. D3Integer remains the mathematical domain 0..2^63-1; bool, floating point, exponent form, negative values, and wrap are forbidden.

NodeRef is WorkspaceId + NodeId. ResourceRef/AnnotationRef encode owner NodeRef plus an owner-local ID. Equality always compares the complete typed ref; bare UUID, path, title, digest, or provider key never participates.

Document has no separate durable ID and is addressed by its owning NodeRef. Document elements, headings, paragraphs, table rows/cells, Field occurrences, and Saved Query/View occurrences are not a fourth entity class.

ForeignIdentityKey, SourceBinding, OriginBinding, Provenance, LogicalOccurrenceKey, ResultRowHandle, ActionEvidence, OperationId, and AuditEventId never coerce to content refs.

## 4. wire12, commit domains, and idempotency ledger

### 4.1 D6 type consumption and D3-native owner input

Question-marked members appear only where the original mode matrix permits them and otherwise are absent. preparationBinding is cross-owner D7 input and is not part of this D3-native descriptor; wire12 top level and the future D7 preparation record bind it separately. ownerInput.pinRefs carry typed slots for large immutable D3-descriptor input and, only on §10.1 resolution, the explicitly guarded canonical input pins bound by the /3 record. Ordinary source/currentness still comes from SourceObservation/1 values in InputDescriptor sourceInputs plus protected pins. The sole conflict-resolution installation-input exception is the differently typed D6 ConflictInstallInput/1 in §10.1.1.2; it stays outside sourceInputs, is bound to the guarded /3 record and never supplies canonical currentness.

`CommitDomain.replica.replicaEpoch` and `CommitDomain.server.authorityInstanceId` continue to be interpreted under the record's original domain profile. The common front gate first proves only current-principal minimum disclosure, authorization applicable to that original profile, `CommitDomain`/backend fence/portable trust, and P-ledger plus custody continuity. Availability or unproved continuity precedes reachable integrity; hidden author/business content, current `SourceObservation/1`, `DependencyProof/2`, or complete ranges are never read first to choose a narrower scope. After the common continuity gate succeeds, Core locates the same `DecisionKey` using the record's actual version, complete original canonical request/fingerprint, and `protocolOwner`, then branches saved/planned/unseen. Only the unseen branch requires current owner version, Observation/dependency/version-basis/Frontier qualification for a new execution; saved/planned are never blocked by placing those current business gates before ledger lookup.

### 4.2 DecisionKey/2 and OperationId ledger

The single durable key for new wire12 decisions is D6 DecisionKey/2. Its workspaceRef is byte-equal to boundWorkspaceRef and its commitDomain/operationId equal the request fields. P continues to index it as:

    (boundWorkspaceRef.workspaceId, D3-CJ/3(commitDomain), operationId)

protocolOwner=D3 means D3 is the primary decision owner for this key; it is not part of DecisionKey and no second D6 primary decision may exist at the same key. The same Workspace/OperationId in different CommitDomains is independent. One DecisionKey has exactly one canonical request fingerprint and one decision.

State remains `unseen -> rejected` or `unseen -> planned -> committed | terminal_failed`. Original saved bytes for `rejected`, `committed`, and `terminal_failed` are immutable, while `planned` only resumes its original frozen plan. After the common continuity/custody gate succeeds and the same key is identified by the record's actual version, complete original request/fingerprint, and `protocolOwner`, exactly three branches exist: a committed/rejected/terminal outcome durably saved by its original protocol follows saved delivery/publication recovery; an existing planned record resumes only that plan; only a truly unseen key enters current producer/consumer business gates and may establish a new plan. A different fingerprint at the same key remains `operation_id_conflict` without reading or disclosing original business content, and preparing a second decision never bypasses it.

inputDescriptor.workspaceRef, commitDomain, expectedFrontier, and guarantee are canonical-equal to top-level values; intentKind identifies D3 identity operation v12. ownerInput is byte-equal to the D3-native descriptor in §4.1, where writeProtection is fixed strict. Large Document/Resource/Annotation bytes use typed PinRef/2 slots only; canonical request never permanently embeds full source bodies or attachments.

Exact input equality compares InputDescriptor, the D3-native owner descriptor, every exact pin, SourceObservation, and DependencyProof. Equal sha256 is neither equal input nor ABA continuity.

#### 4.5.1 create/fork bootstrap domain and custody

For a genuine proposal issued by authority A for fresh target Workspace W and target authority B, `boundWorkspaceRef=W`, `commitDomain={kind:"server",workspaceRef:W,authorityInstanceId:B}`, and `DecisionKey/2={workspaceRef:W,commitDomain,operationId}` bind W/B from the first formal request through rejection, planning, seal, replay, and custody transfer. The same values appear in `InputDescriptor/2` and the receipt/companion. A is the custodian, never a substitute commitDomain; activating B does not re-key the decision. Proposal/framing versions and all original proposal members remain unchanged.

At original stage3, create/fork checks current issuer `allocate_workspace` and authenticated audience before family, target-policy, or target-ledger reads; fork additionally checks its existing source authority/cut and source observation authorization. The temporary `prepared_workspace` qualification is for this proposal's target role. Stage4 qualifies issuer A (and fork source) availability/fence/continuity, not an already active B. The exact sequence is `stage4 -> P1 -> P2 -> TL1 -> TL2 -> stage5`: P1 authenticates the full original proposal/body/audience and W/B/request equality; P2 establishes current proposal or the original same-operation winner/rejected binding. P1/P2 failure is the original `workspace_identity_conflict + preflight_rejection`, with zero target custody/ledger reads and zero family/successor effects.

Only after P2 does TL qualify the unique `TargetLedgerCustodyRecord` for W, its family and custody generation, and issuer-held target ledger/allocation/burn/saved-decision continuity. Unavailable or unproved continuity is `identity_authority_unavailable` before any reachable duplicate/corrupt custody becomes `workspace_integrity_conflict`; neither reads the operation's saved business bytes. The target-domain backend/P fence is proved through this issuer custody, not by requiring B to be active. For an unseen fresh target, the target `expectedFrontier` is the issuer-proved empty target history (not A's Frontier and not an assumed empty store); source fork cuts remain separate. No target `source_write`, current target policy, active entity, or target `policy_admin` is required before creation. Initial creator policy, Registry/Calendar control and source state are derived from the original family-fixed bootstrap profile and validated together before planning. The planning CAS claims the same family and target-domain decision; issuer custody persists while planned or rejected/terminal-before-activation. The single successful seal atomically activates W/B, installs initial control, and hands custody to B. Saved/planned retry retains the original proposal/profile and W/B key and never reinitializes an activated target or replaces its authority binding.

### 4.6 allocation and UUID no-reuse

Within one CommitDomain, Core reserves/burns fresh IDs in P allocation history. Portable metadata records accepted births, tombstones, and no-reuse facts.

If two offline replicas independently mint the same complete typed ref, the receiver never chooses by sync order or hash. It creates identity_collision and no combined canonical entity exists until explicit resolution. Resolution may retain one birth claim and fresh-copy/import the losing branch content under a new ID. It never silently rekeys that branch.

An accepted tombstone ID is never reused. A burned reservation is likewise unavailable for future allocation in its commit domain.

### 4.7 SourceObservation/1, SourceVersion/2, and ABA

D3 separates a persistent Locator's production address from runtime selection/preparation currentness. A managed Locator revision token resolves through D6's canonical RevisionTokenBinding/2 to one exact sealed production SourceVersion/2; selectors, SourceVersionRef, ActionEvidence, Draft maps and prepared evidence still bind the complete D6 `SourceObservation/1`, never a bare revision, digest, I cache, or standalone `SourceVersion/2`. `SourceVersion/2` continues to represent the real source production version: the managed variant retains the production `CommitDomain`, production `observationEpoch`, `revision`, and `changeId`; the external variant retains the production `CommitDomain`, production `observationEpoch`, and `externalSequence`, with no managed revision or `changeId`. The production domain may differ from the current operation `observerDomain` and is never stale merely for that reason; production and current observation epochs remain distinct.

A successful current observation validates every member of the complete `SourceObservation/1`: `kind`, `version`, `observerDomain`, `entityRef`, `sourceVersion`, `observationEpoch`, `fileObjectBinding`, and `evidencePins`. `observerDomain` equals the operation `CommitDomain`, `entityRef` matches the real source owner and `sourceVersion.entityRef`, and the current `observationEpoch`, complete `FileObjectBinding`, protected `evidencePins`, and applicable control/Registry/incidence dependencies belong to the same current cut. Placeholder, missing metadata, a conflict branch, or unproved observation continuity yields no successful Observation. `SourceVersionRef/1` remains only the narrow `{entityRef,sourceToken}` projection; its tag=`d6_source_observation/1` `sourceToken` selects the entire protected Observation and is not a substitute for an inner revision, digest, I cache, or production version.

New-profile revision tokens resolve only through protected `RevisionTokenBinding/2`, whose current shape is `{kind:"d6_revision_token_binding",version:2,token,source}` with no current observerDomain/current observationEpoch. `source` is exactly the closed `RevisionTokenSource/2` managed SourceStamp or complete external SourceVersion/2. For managed source the token is the sole canonical stable production address selected by the original winning plan/seal and authenticated by its sealed-outbox association; loser/aborted/unproved tokens never borrow another seal and later observers never remint it. For external source, reuse requires original protected external-event evidence and never follows from equal bytes. Watcher gaps invalidate old current Observations and runtime selectors/preparations, not the stable managed address itself. A new invocation may use the unchanged managed Locator only after current disclosure/exact-cut gates, canonical binding+seal/outbox validation, a newly established SourceObservation whose complete `sourceVersion` is byte-equal to the resolved production version, exact coordinate/profile validation, and final same-cut revalidation. That new read never mutates or revives old SourceVersionRef/sourceToken, selector, preparation, ActionEvidence, Draft/map or PreparedIntent. Real production-history ABA, unproved rematerialization, bare revision, hash or equal final text remains stale/unavailable. Historical `d6d`/`d6r`/`d6a` profiles, opaque revision decoders, selector/Locator wire and saved bytes retain their original contracts.

For production domain D and entity E, `H(D,E)` is the greatest managed revision of E in D's continuous sealed production history. `H=0` is legal only when that `CommitDomain`'s birth/registration, P continuity, and verified portable sealed history completely prove that D has never sealed a managed source version for E; missing, corrupt, gapped, or otherwise unproved history is not empty history. Every source change that actually produces a managed after uses checked `H+1`; a production `observationEpoch` change never resets H within the same production domain, and `MAX` never wraps. When an existing source is written by another production domain, only that new production domain's H allocates its after revision: a foreign before revision or `externalSequence` never donates a number, and equal revision numbers in different production domains are not equal versions.

Except for the explicitly typed conflict-only /2 source plan in §10.1.1.2, whenever the original plan will produce a new managed after, D3 consumes the D6 `SourceRevisionPlan/1` that freezes the complete version basis for the same `decisionKey`/`entityRef`. A non-absent `before` is the complete current `SourceObservation/1` actually read by that original plan and has `before.entityRef` equal to the plan `entityRef`; `before="absent"` is legal only when the original D3 identity/owner plan proved a genuinely fresh/expected-absent branch and is never inferred from path absence, an I miss, or current invisibility. The proposed `after` `SourceStamp/1.observationEpoch` comes from the production-observation generation that this original plan froze for the target source in the after production domain; a foreign `before.sourceVersion.observationEpoch` never donates that value. For a fresh/expected-absent target, that generation comes from the same plan's verified trusted absent `FileObjectBinding`/target-backend observation generation. The version basis also contains the same-production-domain `lastIssued` managed version or completely proved empty history plus the exact `afterPin`; by entity kind, `afterPin` binds Document owner NodeRef to `exact_source_document`, ResourceRef to `resource_bytes`, or AnnotationRef to `annotation_value`, and the protected pin record binds that same `entityRef`. Digest equality alone never substitutes for this binding. After the winning plan freezes these members, pre-seal validation rechecks the original `lastIssued`/empty-history basis, domain fence, `afterPin`, and all original dependencies. If another managed version for the same production-domain entity has sealed meanwhile, the original plan becomes stale/conflict; Core never edits `after.revision` in place to a newer `H+1`, resamples H, or rewrites the version basis. `SourceRevisionPlan/1` allocates no `ChangeId` for this decision; only seal of an actual source change producing a managed after combines the frozen stamp with the seal-allocated `ChangeId` into `SourceVersion/2`. Source deletion with after=`"absent"`, a true raw no-op, and pure structure/lifecycle/control with unchanged source create no `SourceRevisionPlan/1`. For fresh D3 content, `entityRef` and after come only from the single original stage12 private candidate map. Stage14/15 symbolic materialization, applicable D7 two-pass Q/Locator materialization, and every proposed-position validation use that same candidate map, the same `SourceStamp/1`, and the same revision-token binding. Before any author write, the winning planning CAS must persist that same candidate map as the winning reservation's identity mapping together with the complete `SourceRevisionPlan/1`, pins, and H/empty-history basis and freeze them; identity, revision, and token are never resampled afterward. `SourceStamp/1` is only the original plan's proposed managed-source address: it is not a current `SourceVersion/2`, carries no `ChangeId` for this not-yet-sealed decision, and grants no current Locator capability early. Only after the original decision seals, the real managed `SourceVersion/2` exactly matches that stamp and the actual `ChangeId`, and the complete current Observation remains valid may the associated token qualify for currentness validation.

These currentness and new managed-revision rules govern only new/current consumers using this contract. Historical saved/planned records continue to recover or replay under their original decoders, frozen plan/pins, authorization, and continuity obligations and are never reinterpreted merely because the current Observation, profile, or proof changed. The complete saved/planned/unseen ledger split remains the responsibility of its dedicated submit/recovery ordering. Higher-level `sourceOccurrenceKey` continuity remains owned by D10 and is never inferred from path/hash by D3.

### 4.8 legacy v9/v10/v11

wire9/v10/v11 records and actually existing legacy `PreparedActionBinding/1,/2`, proposal, planned, saved receipt/error/effects, unknown, and recovery records continue only under the decoder, canonical request/fingerprint, authorization, custody, pin-retention, TTL/clock, byte-equivalent replay, and no-duplicate-effect obligations of the contract that actually saved them. The mere presence of a decoder, historical draft, or candidate prose does not prove that a prototype was deployed or make every old surface an active compatibility contract. Conversely, once a saved/planned/unknown record can be proved to exist, its original recovery and retention obligations are not cancelled because deployment evidence for some other prototype is absent or the current producer has advanced.

Forbidden:

- re-encoding an old request as wire12, adding current owner/profile fields, or re-signing it with a new token;
- adding `CommitDomain`, `ChangeId`, `SourceVersion/2`, new companion members, or current-r6 qualification to an old receipt/effects record;
- reinterpreting historical `SourceVersionRef/1`, opaque revision tokens, `PreparedActionBinding/1,/2`, old pins, or old effects under current new decoders;
- using new retention, I rebuild, readable current files, reauthorization, or a newer complete proof to delete an original request/decision/receipt, charge/approval/claim/unknown responsibility, or decision-durable/last-reference evidence promised by the old contract;
- reinterpreting an old strong profile through current `semantic_pending` or a newer narrow local scope;
- treating current files or an equal old pin/hash as proof that lost continuity returned, seal succeeded, or a second decision may be created.

New decisions do not accept legacy wire, and legacy saved/planned records do not accept new semantic fields. If a new contract **explicitly** makes a historical effect expirable and the effect pin is legitimately gone under that contract, delivery of that effect returns its specified `effects_unavailable`; Core never reads current files/current r6 state and pretends they are the historical after. That rule does not weaken old contracts that still promise durable receipt/decision/last-reference pins or charge/approval/claim/unknown responsibility. Real legacy TTL/expiry also remains real: a preparation that never formed a decision and legitimately expired under its original contract is not revived by a new version, while actual planned/saved/unknown records follow their own original recovery rules.

## 5. Workspace, Node, owner, and ordered structure

### 5.1 Workspace and replica

WorkspaceRef remains the identity namespace; replicaEpoch is not Workspace identity. Replica registration preserves WorkspaceId and existing Node/Resource/Annotation refs while adding a new D6 CommitDomain.

Registration does not call continue_workspace, allocate a new WorkspaceId/AuthorityInstanceId, take over global execution responsibility, or assert that an old device stopped.

### 5.2 Node parent/order

The live Node tree remains a connected ordered tree from root. Root parent/ordinal are null; every non-root live Node has exactly one live parent. siblingOrdinal is unique and contiguous from zero within a parent.

D3 remains logical owner of parent/order while D6 portable metadata carries it physically. Path, directory tree, index order, file enumeration, and mtime never become a second owner.

move preserves NodeRef. title/path/label changes do not change child identity. Deleting a parent while retaining children requires an explicit move before deletion and never creates a live orphan.

For `DependencyKey/2.kind=placement_range`, D3 consumes only the D6-frozen closed key, stamp, and evidence carrier; D3 remains the sole owner of complete structure/lifecycle enumeration algorithms. `StructureRange` has exactly nine variants, and every required range is listed as its own key rather than merged through free `closure`, owner JSON, current UI selection, or a generic “all structure” alias:
- `live_children(parentNodeRef)` completely reads that live parent's ordered child list. Positive membership contains every child with its canonical ordinal, while the negative side proves no live child is omitted at the same barrier/cut. Author structure order is preserved rather than Ref-sorted.
- `trash_children(parentNodeRef)` completely reads the canonical Trash child list of that trashed parent. Positive and negative proof jointly cover every child, contiguous unique `trashOrdinal` values, and absence of omitted members; Ref sorting never repairs a corrupt list.
- `trash_roots` completely reads all roots of the canonical Workspace Trash forest in order and proves that no Trash root is omitted.
- `ancestor_chain(nodeRef,forest)` completely proves every parent edge from the subject to the root of the named `live|trash` forest, its boundary, and the no-cycle condition. A missing level, unknown parent, or cycle is not a complete chain.
- `subtree(root,forest)` includes the root and follows every canonical child list through every descendant branch in that forest, with negative proof that no child branch was omitted. Owner-local, reply, and restore membership remain separate ranges.
- `owner_resources(ownerNodeRef)` completely enumerates the owner's Resource directory with each member's real lifecycle/owner facts. It is never filtered by current UI, download state, or source occurrence, and proves that no other owner-local Resource was omitted.
- `owner_annotations(ownerNodeRef)` likewise completely enumerates the owner's Annotation directory and real lifecycle/owner facts; reply closure remains separate.
- `reply_closure(annotationRef)` traverses the D3 same-owner reply relation from the exact Annotation, proving every applicable reply edge/member, same-owner constraint, no-cycle condition, and absence of omitted reply descendants.
- `restore_membership(nodeRef)` reads the canonical portable Trash restore membership for that Node. Membership comes only from the original Trash/lifecycle decision's saved recovery membership and is never reconstructed from equal owner, old original parent, current path/directory, or UI grouping.

Positive members, negative/absence facts, and ordered boundaries are one complete enumeration for each range; “no hit” is negative evidence only after that range's completeness is established. Portable current truth for D3 parent/order, Trash graph, owner-local membership, and reply membership retains one logical owner; index, path, or another sidecar never becomes a second writable structure.

### 5.3 Resource and Annotation owner

Resource/Annotation owner remains immutable. Cross-owner semantics always use fresh-copy. Owner Trash/purge closure, reply acyclicity, and Annotation owner-local target rules remain.

Same-owner Resource byte replacement and Annotation body/target edits preserve refs. A new managed `SourceVersion/2` is produced only when the managed Resource bytes or Annotation value actually changes; a true managed byte/value no-op preserves the existing `SourceVersion/2` and does not advance managed revision merely because the same ref participates in another commit.

### 5.4 structural conflicts

Concurrent placement heads are never repaired automatically by UUID, path, ordinal, mtime, final hash, or a larger sequence. New-FA placement conflicts use D6 `ConflictRecord/2`, but `ConflictSubject`, conflict kind, `ConflictKey/1`, `ConflictId`, and the `D6-ConflictKey/1` hash domain are not versioned by that change. Every member of `ConflictKey/1.heads` is a real verified-continuous sealed `ChangeId` in the same Workspace, and its portable change history proves that head is actually relevant to this key's subjects/kind. Subjects and heads are non-empty, unique, and retain their original canonical ordering. An external race, unknown install, third-state bytes, or any competition without a sealed `ChangeId` remains installation/recovery/conflict evidence and never fabricates a head merely to fit `ConflictKey`.

`ConflictRecord/2.createdAtFrontier` is the real verified-continuous `Frontier/2` at record creation and covers every real key head together with the complete intervening sealed continuity. It proves only the portable causal cut for that conflict record, not complete Query, source materialization, or another owner's positive/negative range. An ordinary resolver still selects no hidden winner without explicit branch/resolution. A missing head, portable-history hole, or corrupt record is never repaired by deleting the unknown branch and returning a smaller “successful conflict.”

A conflict branch can still be opened explicitly and edited, but an unqualified structure read cannot claim one canonical tree.

## 6. Locator, anchor, exact source, and external bytes

DocumentElementLocator, DocumentRangeLocator, ResourceRegionLocator, and l1 retain their wire11 lexical, owner, revision, span/range, base64url, and D3-CJ/3 rules. AuthorAnchorAddress remains a current-revision symbolic address, not an entity.

External physical-invalid or D2-invalid bytes remain the real bytes in the ordinary file, are available through D6 current-source/repair interfaces for read and diagnosis, and are never overwritten by an old pin. A path that actually depends on valid source, a managed inner revision, or complete typed/source proof must stop, repair, or remain unavailable/pending as its contract requires when that evidence is absent. But the mere absence of a managed inner revision never permanently disables an otherwise fully qualified local/ordinary offline structure or lifecycle operation that does not depend on that revision, and unknown source state is never promoted to successful semantics.

## 7. lifecycle: ordinary Trash and strong restore/purge

### 7.1 shared state machine

Node, Resource, and Annotation keep unallocated -> live -> trashed -> live|tombstoned. tombstoned is terminal and Document follows Node.

Trash keeps typed refs, payload, and recovery information. purge removes payload and publishes minimal tombstones that cannot be restored through Weftext.

### 7.2 replica_local Trash

replica_local Trash uses `WriteProtection=strict` and completely proves the local closure actually touched by this operation without presenting local proof as a whole-Workspace complete set. It includes at least:

- the target's exact `lifecycle`, the required `placement_range{subtree(root,forest="live")}`, `live_children` for affected source-parent lists, and `trash_children`/`trash_roots` for destination Trash lists. Every ordered list is complete, contiguous, and unique in the same range proof; pre-existing ordinal corruption still fails closed at the original integrity gate and is never repaired by RefKey sorting;
- the Node subtree moved from live to Trash plus the `owner_resources`, `owner_annotations`, and applicable `reply_closure` members that are actually live and enter this operation's closure. Owner-local/reply members come from real portable directories/graphs rather than UI, path, source occurrence, or owner-equality guesses;
- current lifecycle-write policy for that closure, the actual source/control/foreign dependencies used by the operation, and strict installation qualification for the modified portable metadata components.

When a Node/subtree enters Trash, the newly saved restore membership contains only members in this original closure that actually transition from live to trashed and are intended to restore with that Node. Resource/Annotation objects that were independently trashed earlier retain their independent Trash membership; equal owner never merges them into the later owner-Trash restore membership, re-emits their lifecycle transition, or renumbers them as “deleted by this operation.” Existing sibling/final-index, canonical Trash append/flatten/splice, no-orphan, owner, and reply-acyclic algorithms remain unchanged, and the receipt still lists only the real placement/lifecycle/reference changes required by those rules.

replica_local Trash does not require an ordinary offline delete to pre-scan every other replica or every inbound reference in the Workspace. Only positive/negative ranges actually required and proved by this operation are its local complete evidence. Inbound/cross-object obligations that remain unproved but are explicitly allowed to remain pending by the ordinary profile enter D6 `SemanticState.semantic_pending`, including applicable inbound; the receipt never treats local `referenceLifecycleTransitions`, a local `ref_inbound`, or a Frontier prefix as a Workspace-global enumeration. Missing/partial I, placeholder, unknown state, or a gap likewise never becomes zero inbound.

A later remote source or lifecycle head produces an explicit delete/edit or `lifecycle_concurrent` conflict rather than implicit delete-wins or edit-wins. replica_local scope never rewrites the frozen managed_atomic strong semantics.

### 7.3 managed_atomic Trash

managed_atomic Trash remains fixed to `frontierPolicy=exact` and `WriteProtection=strict`, and may produce `complete_semantics` only after every applicable positive and negative dependency for this operation has been completely proved by its real owner. D3 requires at least target/subtree lifecycle, every affected `live_children`/`trash_children`/`trash_roots` range, owner-local Resource/Annotation directories, reply closure, and complete positive/negative D3 `ref_inbound` enumeration. Zero inbound requires a complete currently authorized directory with a continuous stamp; an I miss, partial shard, placeholder, equal hash, or causal-prefix Frontier never proves it. Concrete foreign/control slots, D4 relation incidence, Registry/Calendar/rule facts, and other constraints continue to come from their own owners; D3 neither copies those algorithms nor substitutes its own range for them.

The strong Trash path retains the complete wire11 suspended/reference-lifecycle, no-orphan, owner/reply, ordered Trash-placement, and compound-effect evidence. If any required complete range is currently unprovable, the managed_atomic operation remains on the applicable `proof_unavailable`, conflict, or availability path. It never automatically downgrades to replica_local, converts unknown range to empty, or uses `semantic_pending` as `complete_semantics`.

### 7.4 restore

restore is managed_atomic only and remains fixed to `frontierPolicy=exact` and `WriteProtection=strict`. It preserves identity and, under current authorization, consumes the canonical Trash graph, restore location, target lifecycle, applicable `trash_children`/`trash_roots`, `subtree(root,forest="trash")`, `restore_membership(nodeRef)`, owner-local Resource/Annotation directories, reply closure, current references, and all other complete required dependencies supplied by their real owners. Every positive and negative range uses its current continuous stamp and is never reconstructed from I, path, title, an old original parent, or current UI state.

When restoring a Node, `restore_membership(nodeRef)` admits only recovery members saved for that Node closure by the original Trash/lifecycle decision, intersected with the current Trash graph/lifecycle to determine which of those members remain restorable. Resource/Annotation objects independently trashed before the owner entered Trash were never members of that owner restore membership and therefore remain trashed when the owner is restored; they are not fabricated as this restore's `already_live`/`tombstoned` omissions. Equal owner alone is never restore membership. The original Node-subtree, Resource/Annotation-owner, Annotation-reply-chain, no-orphan/no-cycle, location-matrix, canonical Trash flatten/splice, and receipt-evidence rules remain unchanged. Independently restored, already restored, or truly tombstoned members continue under the original lifecycle rules and are never automatically reparented, reowned, rekeyed, or fresh-created.

Complete inbound/control/foreign/D4 ranges needed by restore are proved by their actual owners; a D3 `ref_inbound` or placement proof never substitutes for another owner's complete set. If any required range, payload, authorization, or continuity cannot be completely proved, restore does not return empty or strong success. If another replica's edit/move/delete head conflicts with restore, the `ConflictRecord` is formed/resolved and the original exact dependencies are revalidated first. Path guessing, causal-prefix guessing, and silent fresh identity are forbidden.

### 7.5 purge and tombstones

purge is managed_atomic only and remains fixed to `frontierPolicy=exact` and `WriteProtection=strict`. Its closure is deliberately different from restore: restore admits only members saved in the original Node Trash/lifecycle decision's `restore_membership` that are still restorable now, while purge must process every currently trashed member in the target's irreversible owner closure, including Resource/Annotation objects that were independently trashed before their owner and therefore were never part of the owner's restore membership. purge requires at least:

1. the target is already trashed, with current `lifecycle`, complete `subtree(root,forest="trash")`, applicable `trash_children`/`trash_roots`, surviving Trash placement, and every actual owner/reply closure proved at the same exact cut. Existing canonical Trash flatten/splice, contiguous unique `trashOrdinal`, no-orphan/no-cycle, and original-location rules remain; Ref, path, or UI order never repairs them;
2. for a purged Node, complete `owner_resources`, `owner_annotations`, and applicable `reply_closure` cover **every currently trashed owned Resource/Annotation**, including independently trashed members. Existing tombstones retain their original tombstone/no-reuse facts and are never reallocated, revived, or recreated as a second identity;
3. every applicable live inbound reference, complete positive/negative D3 `ref_inbound`, foreign/control dependency, and strong D4 relation-incidence, Registry, Calendar/temporal, D5, or other owner constraint is proved by its real owner under current authorization. `semantic_pending`, replica_local Trash local reference evidence, an I miss, placeholder, partial shard, equal hash/count, or a causal-prefix Frontier never qualifies purge;
4. the relevant state/disclosure/authorization gate succeeds before Core reads any hidden sibling, owner member, reply edge, inbound slot, foreign/control fact, or replica state. Failure follows the existing not-visible/owner error surface and never reveals a negative range, member count, conflict, or emptiness;
5. D6 `replica_registry` proof fixes the complete active/retired `ReplicaRecord` directory, `registrationSequence`, and gap-free continuity, and proves for every active registered replica real admission of the sealed cut required by this purge or an explicit retirement under current policy/trust/execution-responsibility. Currently online replicas, registry count, one stable Frontier, provider “synced”, or a final causal prefix never substitutes;
6. tombstone/no-reuse, allocation/burn history, and applicable completion/publication evidence publish under their original owner contracts. purge never turns old bytes, backup, or later re-entry into identity continuity, and a retired epoch never revives.

A device that was never registered and cannot be proven to have participated in the Workspace is not a permanent blocker. If old directory bytes later re-enter, they register a new `ReplicaEpoch` and reconcile with current tombstones/ConflictRecord; they never resurrect a tombstoned ref. When purge is a lifecycle/portable effect and corresponding source bytes are unchanged, SourceVersion follows the actual source-change rule: pure lifecycle/source-unchanged work creates no new managed source version and does not advance `H(D,E)`, while the real portable purge effect may still receive its ChangeId at seal.

Trashed members never revive into a copy merely because owner or path matches. Every included source payload has a real preimage pin; every mapped result has its real result payload, applicable SourceRevisionPlan when source actually changes, and complete typed-slot coverage. All D2/D3 authored refs/locators and D4 known typed roots follow the existing identity-map/C-carrier rules. If an owner-local member is legally omitted under the original omission algebra, every affected occurrence/reply closure still closes through explicit delete/rewrite/omit evidence; a Node is never dropped under an owner-local omission reason. Complete saved-definition DefinitionTransfer slot/Q details are specified in §21.2 and this section never upgrades CEL/text/free JSON into a typed scan.

### 8.4 Workspace fork and continue

fork/continue are managed_atomic only.

continue is restricted to exclusive continuation/failover/disaster recovery of one authority lineage with complete control ledger, allocation/burn/tombstone history, saved decisions, and fencing of the old authority. Replica registration never uses continue.

fork creates fresh WorkspaceId/AuthorityInstanceId and all mapped content IDs, and source and target never share commit authority. The exact-fork source closure is **every** live and trashed Node/Resource/Annotation in the verified snapshot cut; `identityMap.from` exactly covers that set and no root/member may be omitted. `resultLifecycles` preserves each mapped entity's exact live/trashed state. Live Nodes appear only in `initialPlacements`/the live tree, while trashed Nodes appear only in `trashPlacements`/the canonical Trash forest, forming an exact partition of mapped Nodes. Each trashed Node's `trashParentRef`/`trashOrdinal` mechanically maps the already canonical source-cut Trash graph. A still-mappable original parent produces a mapped original location; purged/tombstoned/not-found original parent produces unavailable original location and never an automatic root attachment. Resource/Annotation ownership, replies, and restore-membership/independent-Trash facts map from the real source cut and are never merged merely because owners match.

Every fork source payload and actual typed ref/locator uses real pins, the same private candidate map, and the applicable `SourceRevisionPlan/1` during validation/materialization. Targets inside the complete live/trashed `identityMap` are rewritten. A source tombstone, not-found target, owner mismatch, or foreign target outside the closure is explicitly transformed or rejects the whole fork; full-fork members are never removed by omission and no foreign/dangling ref commits in the target. Old tombstones are not copied as allocation history for the new Workspace and remain, at most, non-authoritative provenance under the original contract.

There is no cross-Workspace move that preserves NodeRef. An online cross-Workspace copy remains a target fresh-identity copy plus complete identityMap/ref rewrite. If the user also requests source removal, that is a **separate** authorized `trash` operation in the source Workspace. The two Workspaces have distinct DecisionKey/OperationId, authorization, receipt, and failure/recovery state, with no D3 cross-authority atomic commit. Target success followed by an unattempted or failed source delete means only “copied; source not removed”; it never becomes an atomic move, target rollback, or a source-delete claim derived from the target receipt.

#### 8.4.1 Domain-qualified transfer observation

New wire12 coordination evidence uses this D3-owned closed envelope; it is not a receipt or a joint decision:

~~~text
{wireVersion:12,kind:"cross_workspace_transfer_outcome",
 transferOperationId:UUIDv4,observationSequence:D3Integer,
 targetWorkspaceRef:WorkspaceRef,targetDecisionKey:DecisionKey/2,
 targetDisposition:"committed"|"failed",
 sourceWorkspaceRef:WorkspaceRef,sourceDecisionKey?:DecisionKey/2,
 sourceDisposition:"not_attempted"|"pending"|"committed"|"failed"}
~~~

Exactly these members are allowed; every member except conditional `sourceDecisionKey` is required. Unknown/missing/duplicate/null, invalid nested D6 key/domain, or noncanonical UUID/integer rejects. The source/target Workspaces are different and fixed for the transfer; each full key's Workspace and nested commitDomain Workspace equal its corresponding outer Workspace. The target key is fixed from the first observation; the source key is absent exactly for `not_attempted`, required otherwise, and immutable from its first appearance. Both identify the actual `protocolOwner=D3` decisions; wrong owner is not relabeled. Bare `targetOperationId`/`sourceOperationId` members are forbidden in wire12. Equal OperationId UUIDs in different domains never collapse these keys.

`transferOperationId` is a coordination identifier within the selected coordinator's protected record, not a content ref, either ledger key or global registry. `observationSequence` starts at 0 and strictly increases for that record; old observations are immutable, duplicate sequence and identity/key changes reject, and a projection uses only the highest verified observation in that same record. `targetDisposition` is fixed terminal committed/failed and backed by its actual target decision. A failed target requires source not_attempted forever. After a committed target, source progresses only `not_attempted -> pending -> committed|failed`; attempted unknown/planned outcome stays pending, never guessed failed. Terminal states do not regress. At MAX the pair must be terminal (target failed/source not_attempted, or target committed/source committed|failed), and no further observation may be issued; pending or still-actionable not_attempted at MAX is invalid. Current disclosure authorization for both decision/result scopes precedes reading their saved business results; an inaccessible side produces no fabricated failed summary or leaked reason. The summary transports no receipt bodies or hidden detail. Even two committed dispositions mean two independent commits, with no rollback/retry/permission authority. Historical wire11 summary bytes retain their original Workspace-local keys, sequence/state rules and decoder; no CommitDomain is guessed or inserted into old evidence.

### 8.5 import/export re-entry

Formal-backup `continue`/`fork`, full-workspace identity-bearing snapshot, partial identity-bearing bundle, ordinary-format import, and current-Trash restore remain explicitly distinguished by artifact class/mode and are never inferred from path, digest, title, root UUID, filename, or “looks like a backup”.

`continue_workspace` is limited to exclusive continuation/failover/disaster recovery of the same logical Workspace. It proves the original continuation cut and complete allocation/burn/live/Trash/tombstone/decision/P continuity through that cut, and the old authority is stopped or effectively fenced before the new writer obtains write authority.
